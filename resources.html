What is SIWES?

SIWES, the Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme, is a programme that gives students in tertiary institutions practical experience in their field before they graduate. It is coordinated by the Industrial Training Fund (ITF) together with institutions and regulators such as the NUC, NBTE and NCCE. How long your training lasts, and when you take it, depends on your programme and your institution, so confirm the details with your SIWES coordinator.

Getting ready before you apply

Start early. Confirm your SIWES dates with your coordinator, update your CV, and gather the documents you will need, such as your admission letter, student ID and any introduction letter from your school. Decide the kind of work and location you want, and make a list of companies that match. Having everything ready lets you apply as soon as a good opportunity appears.

How to choose a good placement

Look for a company whose work matches your course, so that what you do counts as relevant experience. Ask who will supervise you, what tasks you will handle, and whether the company can sign your logbook and evaluation. Be careful with any placement that asks you to pay a fee to be accepted. On Placewick, companies are verified before they can post.

Writing a CV for a placement

Keep it to one or two pages. Start with your name, contact details, institution, course and level. Add a short profile of two or three lines, then your skills, projects, volunteer work or any previous experience. Use clear headings and plain language, save it as a PDF, and name the file with your name, for example Ada-Obi-CV.pdf.

Writing a short application message

Say who you are, what you study and what placement you are looking for, in three or four sentences. Mention one specific reason you chose the company. Keep a polite, direct tone, check your spelling, and attach your CV. Send each message to the right person rather than copying the same message to everyone.

Preparing for an interview

Read about the company and the role beforehand. Prepare short answers to common questions such as why you want the placement, what you have learned in your course, and a project you are proud of. Arrive on time, dress neatly, and ask at least one question about the work you would do.

Keeping a useful logbook

Write in your logbook every week, not in bulk at the end. Record what you did, what you learned and any problems you solved, in your own words and with enough detail to be specific. Ask your supervisor to review and sign each entry promptly. A complete, signed logbook is part of how your placement is assessed.

Making the most of your placement

Be punctual, ask questions and take notes. Offer to help with tasks beyond the minimum, and keep a record of what you achieve. Near the end, ask your supervisor for feedback and, if appropriate, a reference. Stay in touch with the people you worked with.

Working with your supervisors

You will usually have a workplace supervisor at the company and an academic supervisor from your school. Agree early on how you will report your progress, and tell your academic supervisor where you are placed so a visit can be planned. Raise problems early, politely and in writing where possible.

Hosting students well

Give each student a named supervisor, a clear set of tasks and regular feedback. Sign logbooks every week and complete evaluations on time. Students learn more, and your team benefits from their work. Tell the student early what is expected of them in dress, hours and conduct.

Writing a clear opportunity post

State the role, the skills you need, the location, the duration and whether the placement is paid. Describe the main tasks in plain language and say how students should apply. Clear posts attract better matched applicants and reduce back-and-forth.

Running SIWES for your school

Keep a single record of where every student is placed, assign supervisors by location, and keep your logbook and form records in one place so reports for accreditation are ready when needed. Follow up early with students who have not started applying.

Glossary

Terms you will see on Placewick and in your SIWES paperwork.

SIWES

Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme: practical work experience that students complete as part of their programme.

ITF

Industrial Training Fund, the Nigerian agency that coordinates SIWES.

NUC, NBTE and NCCE

The National Universities Commission, the National Board for Technical Education, and the National Commission for Colleges of Education: the regulators of universities, polytechnics and monotechnics, and colleges of education.

SIWES coordinator

The staff member at an institution who manages SIWES for students.

Workplace supervisor

The person at the company who supervises the student and signs the logbook and evaluation.

Academic supervisor

The staff member from the institution who visits or follows up on the student during the placement.

Logbook

The weekly record of what a student did and learned during the placement.

Form 8

The placement acceptance form for SIWES. Ask your institution for the exact form and requirements for your programme.
